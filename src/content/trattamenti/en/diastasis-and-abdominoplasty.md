Abdominoplasty is the surgical procedure that restores the shape and function of the abdominal wall. It is indicated in the presence of excess skin and fat, loss of tone of the wall or a protruding or pendulous abdomen, conditions that are common after one or more pregnancies or after significant weight loss, including following bariatric surgery. These changes are often associated with diastasis of the rectus muscles, that is, their separation along the midline, and sometimes with umbilical or paraumbilical hernias, and cannot be corrected by diet or physical activity.

Through a horizontal suprapubic incision, plication of the anterior fascia of the rectus muscles is performed, restoring their correct alignment along the midline and the containing function of the wall. At the same time, the excess skin and fatty tissue are removed and the navel is repositioned; any hernias can be treated in the same operation. Each procedure is planned individually, according to the anatomy, the quality of the tissues and the patient’s expectations, with the aim of restoring the outline and function of the abdominal wall.

At the end of the procedure a compression garment is applied, to be worn for at least a month. Recovery at home takes on average 2-3 weeks, during which the stitches are removed. The horizontal scar, whose length varies according to the extent of the excess skin, is generally positioned so that it can be covered by underwear or swimwear, while the scar around the navel is generally barely visible.
