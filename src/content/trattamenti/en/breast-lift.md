Mastopexy is the surgical procedure that lifts and reshapes the ptotic breast, that is, one that has dropped and emptied because of loss of tone in the gland and the skin. Breast ptosis can appear after pregnancy and breastfeeding, following significant weight loss or simply with the passing of the years.

When the gland is well represented, the breast is lifted and reshaped using only the patient’s own tissues. If, on the other hand, the emptying is marked, mastopexy can be combined with the insertion of an implant, to obtain both the lift and the restoration of volume. The type of scar also depends on the degree of ptosis: in milder cases an incision around the areola may be sufficient; in intermediate cases a periareolar and vertical scar is used; in more significant ptosis an inverted-T scar is required, periareolar, vertical and along the inframammary fold.

Each procedure is planned individually, on the basis of the anatomy, the quality of the tissues and the patient’s wishes, with the aim of giving the breast a harmonious and natural shape.

After surgery, a non-wired support bra is worn for about a month. One week of rest and limited use of the arms for about ten days are recommended, and the stitches are removed between the tenth and fourteenth day.
