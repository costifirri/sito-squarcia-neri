Liposuction, or lipoaspiration, is a body-contouring procedure that makes it possible to remove localised fat deposits using specific suction cannulas. The areas most frequently treated are the abdomen, hips, buttocks, thighs, knees and, in the face and neck, the chin and cheeks. In women, fat tends to accumulate on the hips, trochanteric region, abdomen and inner surface of the knees; in men, on the hips, abdomen and chest.

Liposculpture is a variant that also involves more superficial suction, to refine the body outline with greater precision. Depending on the depth of work and the type of cannulas used, a modest skin-retraction effect can also be obtained, useful in cases of mild laxity. In the same session, the fat removed can be purified and regrafted using the lipofilling technique in areas that need volume, such as the buttocks, face and breasts, for a more harmonious silhouette.

Liposuction is not a weight-loss treatment and does not replace diet: it is indicated to harmonise the body’s shape in the presence of resistant localised fat deposits. The procedure is personalised after a thorough consultation, according to the distribution of fatty tissue, the quality of the skin and the patient’s objectives. Depending on the extent of the areas treated, it can be performed under local anaesthesia with sedation or under general anaesthesia.

Normal daily activities can generally be resumed after 24-72 hours, while about two weeks are needed for sport, during which a shaping garment is worn. Swelling, bruising and mild pain are common and resolve spontaneously. Targeted physiotherapy treatments can help to optimise the result in the post-operative period.
