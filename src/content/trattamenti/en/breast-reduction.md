Breast reduction is indicated for patients with excessively large breasts, which can cause pain in the neck, shoulders and back, changes in posture and limitations in physical activity. Very large breasts can also be a source of aesthetic discomfort and affect social life.

The procedure involves removal of excess glandular tissue and skin, reshaping of the breast and repositioning of the areola in proportion to the new shape and volume of the breast. The incisions generally involve the outline of the areola, a vertical line down to the inframammary fold and, in most cases, the fold itself. Over time, and possibly with the help of dedicated treatments, the scars tend to become barely visible.

The extent of the reduction is defined during the preparatory consultations, in agreement with the patient, on the basis of her anatomy, needs and wishes. The aim is a harmonious breast, in proportion with the figure and natural in appearance. Post-operative pain is generally limited and well controlled with common analgesics. In the first month it is advisable to wear a support bra, limit the use of the arms and avoid intense physical activity, to promote proper healing of the scars.
