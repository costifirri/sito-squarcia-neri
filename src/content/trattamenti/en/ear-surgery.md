Otoplasty is the surgical procedure that corrects malformations and imperfections of the outer ear, including prominent or protruding ears, modifying its shape, size and position to bring it into harmony with the face. It is indicated in men, women and children; in childhood in particular, these defects can be a source of psychological and social discomfort.

At around 5-6 years of age the outer ear has reached most of its final size, so from this age the deformity can be considered stable and the procedure can be performed. The greater softness of the cartilage in childhood makes it easier to reshape, but otoplasty also offers good results in adults. The technique is personalised according to the type of defect and the patient’s characteristics.

The procedure is performed as day surgery and lasts about an hour, under local anaesthesia in adults and generally under general anaesthesia in children. At the end a compression dressing is applied, removed after a few days, which reduces swelling and protects the operated area from accidental trauma. An elastic compression band is then worn for about ten days.
